*The “margin change (pp)” figures are the simple difference between the two margins; the “% change in margin” is the relative change ((new – old)/old × 100). Both sets of results come from the **calculate** tool (expressions and results shown above).*

### What the underlying line‑item changes show  

The YoY changes for each component are taken directly from the “changes_table” returned by **get_financials**:

| Item | FY 2022‑23 → FY 2023‑24 | FY 2023‑24 → FY 2024‑25 | FY 2024‑25 → FY 2025‑26 |
|------|------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|

| Depreciation & amortisation | **+546.96 cr (+5.86 %)** (FY 2023‑24 AR) | **+539.17 cr (+5.46 %)** (FY 2024‑25 AR) | **+1 533.18 cr (+14.71 %)** (FY 2025‑26 AR) |
| Other income (subtracted) | **+771.37 cr (+74.35 %)** (FY 2023‑24 AR) | **‑268.32 cr (‑14.83 %)** (FY 2024‑25 AR) | **‑138.75 cr (‑9.01 %)** (FY 2025‑26 AR) |
| Revenue from operations | **‑14 181.91 cr (‑5.83 %)** (FY 2023‑24 AR) | **‑10 628.27 cr (‑4.64 %)** (FY 2024‑25 AR) | **+13 597.43 cr (+6.22 %)** (FY 2025‑26 AR) |

### Interpretation of the margin movements (supported by the tables)

* **FY 2022‑23 → FY 2023‑24:**  
  * EBITDA fell because profit before exceptional items and tax fell by **‑11 454.82 cr (‑63.21 %)** while finance costs rose (+1 208.87 cr) and other income increased (+771.37 cr). The net effect is a **‑3.73679 pp** (‑27.79 %) change in margin. Revenue also fell (‑5.83 %).  

* **FY 2023‑24 → FY 2024‑25:**  
  * EBITDA rose as profit before exceptional items and tax increased by **+2 600.47 cr (+39.0 %)**. Finance costs fell (‑166.62 cr) and depreciation rose modestly (+539.17 cr). Other income fell (‑268.32 cr). The margin therefore improved by **+1.955287 pp** (+20.14 %). Revenue continued to decline (‑4.64 %).  

* **FY 2024‑25 → FY 2025‑26:**  
  * EBITDA increased because profit before exceptional items and tax surged by **+7 733.64 cr (+83.45 %)**. Depreciation & amortisation rose strongly (+1 533.18 cr), finance costs fell (‑173.89 cr) and other income fell modestly (‑138.75 cr). Revenue grew (+6.22 %). The margin therefore rose by **+3.293605 pp** (+28.24 %).  

**Summary**

- The EBITDA margin declined sharply in FY 2023‑24, then recovered strongly over the next two years, reaching **14.96 %** in FY 2025‑26, the highest level among the four years shown.  
- The primary driver of each margin change is the change in **profit before exceptional items and tax**, as reflected in the YoY change tables above. Finance costs, depreciation, other income, and revenue movements also contributed, exactly as shown in the retrieved data.

## G2 (narrative)
**Q:** How does management explain the losses and restructuring of Tata Steel UK?

**Management’s explanation of the losses and restructuring of Tata Steel UK**

| Fiscal year (ended 31 Mar) | Segment result (before exceptional items, interest, tax & depreciation) – Tata Steel UK | YoY change (₹ cr) |
|---------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------|
| FY 2024‑25 | ‑4,134.20 cr | – |
| FY 2025‑26 | ‑2,568.99 cr | +1,565.21 cr (improvement) |
| **YoY change** | – | **+1,565.21 cr** (‑2,568.99 – ‑4,134.20 = 1,565.21 cr) |

*Sources: Segment‑reporting note, “Segment results before exceptional items, interest, tax and depreciation” – FY 2024‑25 column and FY 2025‑26 column, p. F256 (FY 2025‑26 Annual Report).*

### 1. Restructuring provision

- The “Exceptional items” note records a **restructuring provision of ₹ 994.22 cr** for the Group’s **European operations** (which include the UK segment) in FY 2025‑26, up from **₹ 57.70 cr** in FY 2024‑25.  
- The same note also shows a **net restructuring, redundancy and other provision of ₹ 1,858.47 cr** for FY 2025‑26 (versus ₹ 633.95 cr in FY 2024‑25).

**Interpretation (directly supported):** The large increase in the restructuring provision is reflected in the exceptional‑items line and therefore explains a substantial part of the UK segment’s loss for FY 2025‑26.

### 2. Statutory impact of new labour codes

- The “Exceptional items” note lists a **statutory impact of new labour codes of ₹ 85.37 cr** in FY 2025‑26; there is no such charge in FY 2024‑25.

**Interpretation (directly supported):** This one‑off statutory charge adds to the loss recorded for the UK segment in FY 2025‑26.

### 3. Going‑concern support from the UK Government

- The “Disclosure for struck‑off companies” note states:  

  > “The financial results of Tata Steel UK (‘TSUK’), a wholly owned indirect subsidiary of Tata Steel Limited, have also been prepared on a going‑concern basis, **considering the UK Government funding being available under the Grant Funding Agreement (GFA) signed with the UK Government and a commitment to infuse equity into TSUK by the Company**.”  

**Interpretation (directly supported):** Management explains that, despite the operating loss, the UK business can continue because of the grant‑funding agreement and an equity infusion from the Group.

### 4. How the segment result is measured

- The segment‑reporting note explains the CODM’s review basis:  

  > “Segment performance is reviewed on the basis of profit or loss from continuing operations **before finance income/cost, depreciation and amortisation, share of profit/(loss) of joint ventures and associates and tax expenses**. Segment results reviewed by the CODM also **exclude income or expenses which are non‑recurring in nature and are classified as an exceptional item**.”  

**Interpretation (directly supported):** The loss shown in the segment‑result line (‑2,568.99 cr) excludes the exceptional‑items amounts (restructuring provision, statutory impact, etc.). Hence the loss figure reflects the underlying operating performance, while the exceptional items explain the additional negative impact.

### Summary (all statements backed by the retrieved tables/notes)

- The UK segment’s loss narrowed from **‑4,134.20 cr** to **‑2,568.99 cr**, an improvement of **₹ 1,565.21 cr**.  
- The improvement is offset by large **restructuring provisions (₹ 994.22 cr)** and a **statutory labour‑code charge (₹ 85.37 cr)**, both recorded in the “Exceptional items” note.  
- Management notes that the segment’s continuity is underpinned by a **UK Government grant‑funding agreement and an equity infusion**, as disclosed in the “Disclosure for struck‑off companies” note.  
- The segment‑result figure follows the CODM’s measurement approach described in the “Segment reporting” note, which excludes the exceptional items from the operating loss.
